about

I've always wanted to know how things work underneath, whether that's an old engine, a house, or the computer on my desk. A few years ago I turned that curiosity toward the systems I relied on every day and started learning them for myself.

That began with Linux administration. The more time I spent configuring and breaking my own machines, the more I wanted to understand how they could be attacked and defended, which eventually led me to earn my Security+. Today I run my own homelab and network, and I'm drawn to lightweight, minimal systems: low-overhead architectures, custom workflows, and clean codebases I can actually read end to end. I'd rather build something small that does exactly what I need than inherit a heavy framework I only half understand.

These days I'm learning Rust alongside a long-term study plan in reverse engineering and malware analysis, starting from the fundamentals of how binaries are put together. It's slow, deliberate work, and I document what I learn as I go.

I also give community talks on scams and online privacy, where I focus on taking topics that feel confusing or intimidating and making them clear enough to actually use.
